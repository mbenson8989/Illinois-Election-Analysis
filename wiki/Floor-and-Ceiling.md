# Floor and Ceiling Counties

> **\*2026 figures are UNOFFICIAL (pre-Dec 4, 2026 canvass), not certified by the Illinois State Board of Elections.** Governor race unless a page says otherwise. Votes are votes for the Democratic candidate. **Registration stand-in: Democratic primary ballot share - NOT official party registration; Illinois has no party registration:** R and D on these pages are Democratic primary ballots ÷ all primary ballots × 100 (SBOE official primary canvass), not a count of registered Democrats. These pages measure a pattern; they make no claim about intent, fraud or cause.

## How to read floor and ceiling counties

Look for the pattern at both ends. The ceiling counties have the highest ΔG; the floor counties have the lowest. These are the 80 counties with 2026\* data.

- **The floor** is the single county with the lowest ΔG in the state. In Illinois that's McHenry, at -22.05\*.
- **The ceiling** is the single county with the highest ΔG in the state. In Illinois that's Gallatin, at +23.01\*.

Every state will have its own floor and ceiling. Those two extremes set the range the rest of that state's counties fall within. ΔG is positive in 10 of 80 Illinois counties, because the stand-in (R) rose in most counties.

## Terminology

- **Ceiling counties** have the highest ΔG: Gallatin +23.01\*, Mercer +6.31\*, Cass +5.46\*, McDonough +3.87\*, Edgar +3.42\*. Gallatin's ΔG is driven by its stand-in collapsing from 44.69% to 21.56%; its 2022 Democratic primary ran unusually high for the county.
- **Floor** counties have the lowest ΔG: McHenry -22.05\*, Lee -19.55\*, Boone -19.14\*, Kankakee -18.85\*, DuPage -17.98\*.
- **Non-loss counties** (also called **share-gain counties**) had no stand-in Democratic headcount loss. They get raw numbers only, with no residual step. That is 76 of 80 counties; the exceptions are Mercer, Moultrie, Hamilton, Gallatin.
- **Baseline floor and ceiling** (original Illinois spec): the lowest and highest Democratic share of ballots cast. See below.
